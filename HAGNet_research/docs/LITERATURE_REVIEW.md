# Literature Review — Lesion-Resolved Sleep Microstructure in Ischemic Stroke

> Structured review grounding the proposed study. Organized by theme; each ends with the
> implication for our work. Crowdedness verdict per theme: CROWDED / EMERGING / OPEN.
> (Compiled June 2026 from a targeted multi-database survey.)

---

## 1. Automatic sleep staging — a mature, saturated *tool*  ·  CROWDED

Automatic sleep staging from EEG is one of the most worked problems in biomedical signal
processing. Established backbones span CNN+RNN (DeepSleepNet, Supratak et al., *IEEE TNSRE*
2017; TinySleepNet, Supratak & Guo 2020), sequence models (SeqSleepNet, Phan et al. 2019;
L-SeqSleepNet 2023), and fully-convolutional multi-cohort systems (U-Sleep, Perslev et al.,
*npj Digital Medicine* 2021), now a standard clinical baseline. State-of-the-art accuracy
sits around 85% and reviews argue the field is **approaching a practical ceiling** set by
inter-scorer aleatoric uncertainty (systematic review, arXiv:2405.11008, 2024). Two open
sub-threads remain: (i) **generalization / domain shift** across channels and populations,
where transfer "performance improvement is limited" (Guillot et al., *Front. Physiol.* 2023;
domain-invariant learning, arXiv:2312.03196); SeqSleepNet in particular is "sensitive to
small datasets." (ii) **Uncertainty estimation** (DeepSleepNet-Lite, 2021) and
**interpretability** (SleepTransformer; WaveSleepNet 2024), motivated by clinical trust.

> **Implication.** Building a better stager is crowded and not a defensible contribution.
> We use a competent, subject-independent stager strictly as an *instrument*; our pilot
> already reproduces the published CNN (0.613 vs 0.617). Classical features + boosting
> matched deep nets on our small pilot, consistent with the small-data caveats above.

## 2. Sleep macro-architecture after stroke  ·  EMERGING

Stroke reliably distorts sleep *architecture*: increased N1/N2 and arousal index, **reduced
slow-wave sleep**, with no change in total sleep time vs controls (Bassetti & Aldrich,
*Sleep* / *Neurology*; Egyptian PSG cohort, *BMC Neurology* 2023; acute-stroke architecture,
*Sci. Rep.* 2025, s41598-025-01619-6). N1 and REM are **negatively associated with stroke
severity**, and architecture correlates with outcome. Lesion *site* matters at the macro
level: **infratentorial (brainstem/cerebellar) stroke shows poorer sleep efficiency, reduced
REM, and higher AHI** than supratentorial (Hermann/Bassetti group; *Sleep Medicine* 2021;
SAS-CARE-1, *Sleep & Breathing* 2025). A major **confounder**: 70-85% of ischemic-stroke
patients have sleep-disordered breathing (meta-analysis, *Sleep Med Rev*); in iSLEEPS itself
**85% have some apnea** (Mild 24% / Moderate 23% / Severe 38%) (Maiti et al., *Sci. Data*
2026), and SDB independently fragments architecture.

> **Implication.** Macro-architecture-by-site is partly mapped; our novelty is at the
> *microstructure* (spindle/slow-wave) and *lateralization* level, at scale, with ML. AHI
> MUST be a covariate — it is the dominant confound in this cohort.

## 3. Sleep microstructure after stroke: spindles & slow waves  ·  OPEN (mechanistically grounded, contested)

**Mechanism.** Sleep spindles (sigma, 11-16 Hz, N2) are generated by the **thalamic
reticular nucleus (TRN)** pacemaker interacting with thalamocortical relay cells and
corticothalamic feedback (Fernandez & Lüthi, *Physiological Reviews* 2019); core vs matrix
thalamocortical pathways shape spindle topography. Slow waves (delta, <4 Hz, N3) are
cortically generated but thalamus-modulated. **Because these are network-generated, focal
lesions should disrupt them in a spatially specific way** — the premise of our study.

**Empirical, post-stroke.** Acute hemispheric stroke alters sleep EEG, with an **increase in
slow oscillations / delta over the lesioned hemisphere** and changes that correlate with
**stroke volume and outcome** (Gottselig et al., *Neuroscience* 2002; Bassetti, acute
hemispheric stroke EEG). **Slow-wave sleep is causally tied to recovery**: optogenetically
evoked slow waves in the peri-infarct zone improved motor recovery and drove axonal
sprouting (Facchin et al., *J. Neurosci.* 2020); slow-wave + spindle + GABA dynamics underpin
post-stroke memory consolidation (PMC9281513, 2022). Glymphatic clearance tracks delta power,
though NE-driven micro-arousals may matter more (Cell 2024).

**The CONTESTED question — lateralization.** Whether disruption is *ipsilesional* is
unresolved:
- *Pro:* "Disturbed laterality of NREM sleep oscillations" post-stroke — pronounced
  hemispheric asymmetry in slow oscillations, delta, and spindles (pilot, *Sleep*-adjacent,
  PMC10187327, 2023); ipsilesional reduction of spindle amplitude and corticothalamic
  activation (simultaneous EEG-MEG, Gottselig 2002).
- *Con:* "**Unilateral thalamic stroke does NOT decrease ipsilateral sleep spindles**"
  (Santamaria et al., PubMed 10811377); high-density spindle detection found only **left-
  sided** damage reduced (posterior/fast) spindle power, with spindles "only mildly laterally
  affected" (*Sci. Rep.* 2018, PMC6294746).

> **Implication.** This is the open scientific core. Prior studies are N≈12-23, HD-EEG,
> single-site, with directly opposing conclusions. A 100-patient cohort with a within-subject
> ipsilesional-vs-contralesional design can **adjudicate** it. Our pilot (N=28) already shows
> a significant **ipsilesional spindle reduction (sigma N2, Wilcoxon p=0.038)** and an
> **ipsilesional delta increase (N3, p=0.056 trend)** — both directionally consistent with
> the "pro" and acute-delta literature.

## 4. Lesion-resolved / topographic effects on sleep  ·  OPEN (literature-stated gap)

Specific topographies have specific signatures: **pontine/brainstem** lesions cause severe
**REM (and NREM) reduction** and loss of REM atonia, with lesion-overlap maps in medioventral
pons (Tellenbach et al., *J. Sleep Research* 2023; classic four-case series, *Neurology* 1988);
**thalamic** lesions modulate spindles and slow-wave renormalization, with **mediodorsal**
nucleus involvement linked to reduced spindle/slow-wave amplitude and altered REM (thalamic
spindle study, *Sci. Rep.* 2018; Thalamic Stroke & Sleep Study, *Stroke* 2024). That same
work **explicitly calls** for "lesion-symptom mapping to clarify differential effects of
infarct topography on sleep parameters." No study has done this at cohort scale with
automated quantification.

> **Implication.** RQ2 (topography → which features/stages break) fills an explicitly stated
> gap. iSLEEPS provides lesion side (~60%) and territory (~56%), enabling at least a
> hemisphere + broad-territory mapping that prior small studies could not.

## 5. Sleep / EEG biomarkers of stroke severity and outcome  ·  EMERGING

Asymmetry-based **resting** qEEG is an established prognostic: the (pairwise-derived) **Brain
Symmetry Index correlates with mRS at 6 months** and, with NIHSS, independently predicts
disability (Agius Anastasi et al., *Stroke Res. Treat.* 2017; revised BSI, PubMed 27584547;
qEEG 6-month outcome, *Clin. Neurophysiol.* 2010; *Front. Neurosci.* 2020). On the **sleep**
side: NREM/wake **slow-wave-activity ratio correlates with NIHSS (r≈-0.47)**; spindle density
predicts subacute motor recovery; sleep-health parameters relate to functional recovery
(Denis et al., *J. Sleep Research* 2024). A 2025 retrospective (314 patients; 229 stroke)
links spindle number/amplitude/duration to post-stroke **cognition** (*Sci. Rep.* 2025).

> **Implication.** Asymmetry-as-biomarker is validated for *resting* qEEG, not *sleep*
> microstructure; sleep biomarkers exist but are not lateralized or lesion-resolved. RQ3
> extends the asymmetry-biomarker idea into sleep, and adds a novel **"model-surprise"** term.
> Our pilot already shows asymmetry scales with NIHSS (ρ=0.50, p=0.006).

## 6. Model-as-instrument (classifier behavior as a biomarker)  ·  OPEN

Interpretable staging (SleepTransformer; WaveSleepNet) and entropy-based **deferral of
low-confidence epochs** exist, but are aimed at clinical *trust/adoption*, not at *measuring
pathology*. No work uses a sleep stager's **error structure or per-patient representational
shift as a quantitative biomarker** of stroke. Our pilot domain-gap result (healthy→stroke,
per-stage recall collapse, REM 0.90→0.14) is a first step toward treating "where/how the
model fails" as data about the injured brain.

> **Implication.** Genuinely open. We frame the model's per-patient uncertainty / domain
> shift as a candidate severity marker (RQ3), with the montage confound explicitly controlled.

## 7. iSLEEPS-specific work  ·  OPEN (brand new)

Only the data descriptor exists (Maiti et al., *Sci. Data* 2026): 100 subacute ischemic-stroke
patients (mean age 50.5; 77M/23F), 6 EEG channels (mixed M1/M2 and A1/A2 references), 128→100 Hz,
95,305 epochs (W 26.2 / N1 9.9 / N2 41.3 / N3 8.7 / REM 11.8 %). Published **subject-independent**
baselines (patient-exclusive 10-fold): **LSTM 74.70% (macro-F1 67.68, κ 0.64)**, Transformer
67.44%, CNN-ResNet18 61.65%. No downstream studies yet.

> **Implication.** The 74.70% is a *legitimate subject-independent* benchmark, so our gap on
> the 39-subject pilot is a data-quantity effect (now addressed with the full cohort), not a
> protocol artifact. We adopt the same patient-exclusive protocol for comparability.

## 8. Synthesis — the precise gap and our position

| Theme | Verdict | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep staging (healthy) | CROWDED | nothing for us to add; use as instrument |
| Macro-architecture in stroke | EMERGING | mapped at site level; not microstructure/lateralization at scale |
| Microstructure (spindle/SW) post-stroke | OPEN, **contested** | adjudication at N>50 with within-subject design |
| Lesion-symptom mapping of sleep | OPEN (stated) | no cohort-scale automated mapping |
| Sleep/EEG severity-outcome biomarker | EMERGING | not sleep-lateralized; no model-derived marker |
| Model-as-instrument | OPEN | nobody uses staging-model behavior as a stroke biomarker |
| iSLEEPS downstream | OPEN | first study |

**The defensible Q1 contribution** sits at the intersection of rows 3-6 on the iSLEEPS cohort:
a **first large-cohort, lesion-resolved characterization of sleep-microstructure disruption in
ischemic stroke**, that (a) **adjudicates the contested ipsilesional-asymmetry question** with a
within-subject design, (b) **maps disruption to lesion topography** (the stated gap), and (c)
tests **sleep-microstructure + model-surprise as a severity/outcome biomarker** — with automated
staging as the enabling instrument, not the claim. The mechanism (TRN/thalamocortical spindle
generation; slow-wave-driven plasticity) gives it a principled, non-incremental backbone.

**What is required to clear Q1 (not read as incremental):**
1. Full cohort (N≈97 usable) + same subject-independent protocol as the descriptor.
2. Automated **spindle and slow-wave detection** (not just stage labels).
3. **Within-subject** ipsilesional-vs-contralesional contrast + **mixed-effects** models,
   effect sizes, multiple-comparison correction.
4. **Control AHI** (85% apneic here) and the **montage confound** (M1/M2 vs A1/A2; frontal vs
   central for the healthy reference); honest missingness handling.
5. Headline a **clinical-neuroscience finding + candidate biomarker**, never an accuracy number.
6. State the ceiling: cross-sectional, single-night, single-scorer, subacute, moderate
   lesion-localization granularity → associations, not causation; hypothesis-generating at scale.

**Target Q1 journals:** *Sleep*, *Journal of Sleep Research*, *NeuroImage: Clinical* (clinical
framing); *IEEE J-BHI*, *Computers in Biology and Medicine* (methods + biomarker).
