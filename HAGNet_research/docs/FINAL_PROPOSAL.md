# Research Proposal

## Lesion-Resolved Sleep Microstructure in Ischemic Stroke: Mapping How Focal Brain Injury Reorganizes Sleep, Using Automated EEG Analysis as an Instrument

**Status:** pre-implementation research proposal · single-dataset, fully offline, subject-independent EEG study
**Target dataset:** iSLEEPS (Maiti et al., *Nature Scientific Data*, 2026) — 100 polysomnography recordings of ischemic-stroke patients
**Companion documents:** [LITERATURE_REVIEW.md](LITERATURE_REVIEW.md) (full survey) · [PROPOSAL_Q1.md](PROPOSAL_Q1.md) (positioning) · [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) (pilot results)

---

## Abstract

Automatic sleep staging is a mature, near-saturated problem on healthy sleepers, but it is
not the scientific question that the new iSLEEPS stroke cohort makes possible. We propose to
use a validated, subject-independent automatic sleep stager **as an instrument** to produce
the first large-cohort, **lesion-resolved characterization of how ischemic stroke reorganizes
sleep microstructure**. Three questions drive the work: whether disruption of sleep spindles
and slow waves is greater **ipsilesionally** (a question the small prior literature directly
contradicts itself on); whether **lesion topography** predicts which sleep features and stages
break (an explicitly stated gap); and whether these signatures — together with the model's
own per-patient uncertainty — constitute a **biomarker of stroke severity and outcome**. A
within-subject ipsilesional-versus-contralesional design, mixed-effects statistics, and
control of the dominant confounders (sleep-disordered breathing, montage) make the result
defensible at a Q1 venue. Preliminary analysis on the cohort (N = 43 unilateral strokes)
shows a directional ipsilesional reduction of spindle-band activity (a trend, Wilcoxon
p ≈ 0.07) and, more robustly, that the **spindle asymmetry scales with NIHSS severity**
(Spearman ρ = 0.41, p = 0.006), grounded in the thalamocortical mechanism of spindle
generation. The contribution is a clinical-neuroscience finding and a candidate biomarker,
not an incremental classifier.

## 1. Background and motivation

Sleep is not a passive bystander in stroke recovery. Slow-wave sleep supports synaptic
plasticity and metabolite clearance, and experimentally enhancing peri-infarct slow waves
improves motor recovery and drives axonal sprouting (Facchin et al., *J. Neurosci.* 2020);
sleep-disordered breathing, present in the large majority of stroke patients, independently
worsens outcome. Reliable measurement of sleep in this population is therefore a clinical
prerequisite, yet automatic sleep staging — though excellent on healthy sleepers — degrades
when the underlying neurophysiology is altered, precisely because stroke distorts the
features (spindles, slow waves, hemispheric symmetry) that scorers and models depend on.

Crucially, the *interesting* problem is not "stage sleep slightly better." Sleep spindles are
generated by a thalamocortical circuit centred on the thalamic reticular nucleus (Fernandez &
Lüthi, *Physiological Reviews* 2019), and slow waves are cortically generated but
thalamus-modulated. Because these rhythms are **network-generated**, a focal lesion should
perturb them in a **spatially specific, severity-graded** way. That makes the EEG signature of
disrupted sleep a potential *readout of the injured network* — and makes an accurate sleep
stager a measuring instrument rather than an end in itself. This reframing is the core of the
proposal.

## 2. Literature review and the gap

A full thematic review with citations is provided in [LITERATURE_REVIEW.md](LITERATURE_REVIEW.md);
the essentials and the resulting crowdedness verdict are summarised here.

- **Automatic sleep staging (healthy) — saturated.** Established backbones (DeepSleepNet,
  SeqSleepNet, TinySleepNet, U-Sleep) sit near an ~85% accuracy ceiling set by inter-scorer
  uncertainty. Open threads (domain shift, uncertainty, interpretability) are engineering, not
  clinical discovery. *We use this only as the instrument.*
- **Sleep macro-architecture after stroke — emerging.** Stroke reduces slow-wave and REM
  sleep and raises arousals; infratentorial lesions are worse than supratentorial; N1/REM scale
  inversely with severity. This is partly mapped at the *macro* level.
- **Sleep microstructure after stroke — open and contested.** Whether spindle/slow-wave
  disruption is *ipsilesional* is unresolved: some report disturbed laterality and ipsilesional
  spindle reduction (PMC10187327, 2023; Gottselig et al., *Neuroscience* 2002), while others
  find unilateral thalamic stroke does **not** reduce ipsilateral spindles (Santamaria et al.)
  and only left-sided damage reduces (fast) spindle power (*Sci. Rep.* 2018). These studies are
  N ≈ 12–23, high-density EEG, single-site, with opposing conclusions.
- **Lesion-symptom mapping of sleep — open, explicitly requested.** Brainstem lesions abolish
  REM; thalamic (mediodorsal) lesions reduce spindles. The thalamic-spindle study *explicitly
  calls for* cohort-scale "lesion-symptom mapping … of infarct topography on sleep parameters."
- **Sleep/EEG biomarkers of severity and outcome — emerging.** Resting quantitative-EEG
  asymmetry (Brain Symmetry Index) predicts mRS at six months; sleep slow-wave-activity ratio
  correlates with NIHSS; spindle density tracks motor recovery. None is *sleep-lateralized* or
  *lesion-resolved* at scale.
- **Model-as-instrument — open.** Interpretable staging exists for clinical trust, but nobody
  uses a stager's error structure or representational shift as a *biomarker of pathology*.

**The gap.** No study has produced a large-cohort, lesion-resolved map of sleep-microstructure
disruption in stroke that adjudicates the ipsilesional question, links signatures to lesion
topography, and tests them as a severity biomarker. iSLEEPS (100 patients, lesion metadata,
NIHSS/mRS) uniquely enables it, and it is brand new with no downstream work.

## 3. Research questions and hypotheses

- **RQ1 — Lateralization (adjudicate a contested result).** Within patients, is sleep-
  microstructure disruption greater on the **ipsilesional** hemisphere?
  *H1: ipsilesional reduction of spindle activity and an ipsilesional increase of slow/delta
  activity, modulated by territory.*
- **RQ2 — Topography (fill a stated gap).** Does lesion location/territory (cortical vs
  subcortical/thalamic; vascular territory) predict **which** sleep features and stages are
  most disrupted? *H2: subcortical/thalamic lesions → spindle and N2/N3 loss; brainstem →
  REM loss; cortical → slow-wave changes.*
- **RQ3 — Biomarker.** Do microstructure signatures, and the stager's **per-patient
  uncertainty/representational shift**, scale with NIHSS and relate to mRS/Barthel?
  *H3: greater disruption and greater "model surprise" with greater severity.*
- **RQ4 — Instrument feasibility (not the contribution).** Can a subject-independent automated
  pipeline measure all of the above reliably under altered stroke physiology? *Pilot: yes.*

## 4. Dataset

iSLEEPS comprises **100 ischemic-stroke patients** (mean age 50.5; 77 M / 23 F) recorded within
one month of onset (subacute). Each has full overnight PSG: 6 EEG channels (mixed M1/M2 and
A1/A2 references; 128 → 100 Hz), EOG, EMG, ECG, respiration, SpO₂, with manual AASM 30-s
staging (95,305 epochs; W 26.2 / N1 9.9 / N2 41.3 / N3 8.7 / REM 11.8 %). A
`subject_description.xlsx` provides **63 clinical columns**: lesion side (~60% populated),
vascular territory (~56%), and **NIHSS / mRS / Barthel (99%)** and **AHI (100%)**. Apnea is
pervasive: Mild 24% / Moderate 23% / Severe 38% (i.e. ~85% apneic). Published
**subject-independent** baselines (patient-exclusive 10-fold) are LSTM 74.70% (macro-F1 67.68,
κ 0.64), Transformer 67.44%, CNN-ResNet18 61.65%.

**Problem type.** Supervised 5-class classification (the staging instrument); the science is the
downstream lesion-resolved analysis.

## 5. Methodology

**Instrument.** A compact, subject-independent automatic stager (validated in our pilot to
reproduce the published CNN), adopting the descriptor's patient-exclusive protocol for direct
comparability. Architecture is intentionally *not* the contribution.

**Microstructure quantification.** Beyond stage labels, automated detection of **sleep spindles**
(sigma 11–16 Hz, N2; event density, amplitude, duration) and **slow-wave activity** (delta
0.5–4 Hz, N3), per channel, using established detectors (e.g. A7/YASA-style) plus the
band-power proxies used in the pilot.

**Design — within-subject lateralization.** Each patient's right-hemisphere derivations
(C4, O2) and left-hemisphere derivations (C3, O1) are labelled ipsilesional/contralesional from
the lesion side, and contrasted **within subject** (paired) — controlling for inter-subject
variability, the single biggest methodological strength.

**Lesion-resolved stratification.** By hemisphere, vascular territory, and cortical vs
subcortical, with brainstem cases examined for REM-specific effects.

**Clinical correlation.** Microstructure asymmetry and model uncertainty against NIHSS and
mRS/Barthel.

**Statistical rigour and controls (what makes it Q1).** Linear **mixed-effects models** with
patient as a random effect; effect sizes and confidence intervals; multiple-comparison
correction. **AHI as a covariate in every model** (the dominant confounder here). A **healthy
reference** (Sleep-EDF) for the normal asymmetry baseline, with the **frontal-vs-central montage
confound** explicitly harmonised and reported. Honest missing-data handling and sensitivity
analyses. **Interpretability** (attention/Grad-CAM) to tie model decisions back to spindle/slow-
wave structure.

## 6. Preliminary results (feasibility)

Two pilot results establish feasibility and de-risk the proposal.

**(a) The instrument is valid.** On the open 40-subject subset (N = 39, subject-independent
5-fold CV), our per-epoch CNN reaches 0.613 accuracy — essentially the published CNN's 0.617 —
confirming the pipeline. The best honest configuration (gradient-boosted features + HMM temporal
smoothing) reaches 0.663; the gap to the published 0.747 is a **data-quantity** effect (39 vs
100 patients), now addressed with the full cohort. Notably, on this small sample classical
feature-based models matched deep nets, consistent with the small-data literature.

**(b) First lesion-resolved signal.** A within-subject ipsilesional-vs-contralesional analysis
on the full cohort (spindle/N2 analysis N = 43 unilateral strokes, 32 right / 11 left; slow-wave/
N3 analysis N = 35, limited because many patients lack sufficient deep sleep) shows a
**directional ipsilesional reduction of spindle-band activity** that is a trend at this N
(Wilcoxon p ≈ 0.07), and — robustly — that the **spindle-band asymmetry scales with NIHSS
severity** (Spearman ρ = 0.41, **p = 0.006**). In other words, the lateralized disruption is not
uniform but graded by stroke severity, consistent with the literature that large strokes
globalize the deficit. This directly supports RQ3 (a severity biomarker) and lends directional
support to RQ1. The definitive test of the paired ipsilesional effect will use **event-based
spindle detection** (density/amplitude/duration, not a band-power proxy), **mixed-effects models**
with AHI as a covariate, and topographic stratification.

## 7. Expected contributions

1. The **first large-cohort, lesion-resolved map** of sleep-microstructure disruption in
   ischemic stroke.
2. **Adjudication** of the contested ipsilesional-asymmetry question with a within-subject
   design at 2–4× the sample of prior work.
3. A **topographic** account linking lesion location to specific sleep-feature/stage failures
   (fills a literature-stated gap).
4. A **candidate EEG sleep biomarker** of stroke severity, including a novel "model-surprise"
   term.
5. A **reproducible pipeline** and the first downstream study on iSLEEPS.

## 8. Work plan / timeline

- **Phase 0 — Setup (done).** Data acquisition (full 100-subject cohort), montage harmonisation,
  metadata audit, duplicate detection, preprocessing to 30-s epochs.
- **Phase 1 — Instrument & E1/E2 (pilot done; scaling).** Full-cohort subject-independent
  staging; within-subject ipsilesional analysis (RQ1) with mixed-effects.
- **Phase 2 — Contributions.** Topographic mapping (RQ2); severity/outcome biomarker (RQ3);
  interpretability and full confound control (E4/E5).
- **Phase 3 — Writing & submission.** Methods, results, figures, limitations; submit to target
  venue.

## 9. Limitations (stated up front)

iSLEEPS is **cross-sectional, single-night, single-scorer, subacute**, with **moderate
lesion-localisation granularity** (territory ~56%, fine region ~22%). Therefore: associations,
not causation; "biomarker" means a cross-sectional association, not a validated longitudinal
predictor; topography will likely resolve hemisphere and broad territory, not fine networks.
Effect sizes will be modest — the value is a **rigorous, scaled, hypothesis-generating**
characterisation, the largest of its kind. The timing of mRS/Barthel (admission vs follow-up)
will be verified before any outcome claim. These are disclosed, not hidden.
