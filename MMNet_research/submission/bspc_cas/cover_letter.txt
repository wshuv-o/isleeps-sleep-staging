Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript "MM-Net: A Multimodal Multi-Task Network for Sleep Staging and Sleep
Apnea Assessment After Ischemic Stroke" for consideration as a Research Paper in Biomedical
Signal Processing and Control.

The paper addresses a signal-processing problem that arises whenever a pretrained physiological
encoder is adapted to a clinical cohort and then feeds a second model. Adapting the LaBraM EEG
encoder to post-stroke sleep improves the encoder itself, yet the adapted encoder adds nothing
to the downstream model while its training embeddings come from encoders whose coordinates differ
from those of the encoder used at test time. We integrate the adapted encoder through cross-fitting
and ridge alignment to the deployed embedding space. With alignment the downstream gain appears in
eight of ten patient-independent folds, against five of ten without it (Table 6, Figure 5).

Built on this encoder, MM-Net combines EEG/EOG representations with a learned cardiorespiratory
stream and stages sleep, detects respiratory events, and estimates the apnea-hypopnea index from
one overnight recording:

 - iSLEEPS (99 patients with subacute ischemic stroke): 76.3% staging accuracy (Cohen's kappa
   0.656) and 0.825 respiratory-event AUC, ahead of thirteen staging architectures retrained on
   the same patient-independent folds (Holm-corrected p <= 0.025 for every comparison).
 - Joint training improves on matched single-task models for both outputs, and modality removal
   shows that each stream carries the output closest to its physiology.
 - ISRUC-I (sleep disorders): 80.3% accuracy and 0.889 respiratory AUC; Sleep-EDF (healthy
   sleep): 86.4% accuracy.
 - Trained on iSLEEPS and applied without retraining, respiratory detection reaches 0.833 AUC
   on ISRUC-I and 0.841 AUC on the UCD apnea database.

The published work on the iSLEEPS cohort (Maiti et al., Scientific Data 2026; Bose et al., ISBI
2026; Alhazmi, Brain Sciences 2026) addresses sleep staging only. To our knowledge, MM-Net is the
first framework to analyse sleep architecture and respiratory events jointly in this population,
and the integration procedure applies to any pipeline that fine-tunes a foundation model and trains
a second model on its outputs. We believe the work fits the journal's scope in multimodal
physiological signal processing and clinically oriented machine learning.

All datasets are public, and the code, per-fold configurations and reproduction notebooks are
released at https://github.com/wshuv-o/MM-Net.

This manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration for
publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and declare no competing
interests.
